media: cedrus: reconstruct 10-bit pictures into AFBC for P010 output

With P010 capture the decoder writes the userspace picture through the
secondary output engine and keeps its reconstruction, which later
pictures use as reference, in a hidden surface behind it. That surface
was linear NV12 plus the appended low-2-bit plane.

Linear references are slow on the H616. Measured on an Orange Pi
Zero2 (DDR3-1440, VE 600 MHz) with 4K HEVC Main10, the reference-read
port (MBUS VE1, IOMMU master 2) moves 3.2 times the data of an AFBC
reconstruction and 1.7 times that of a tiled one, and its IOMMU TLB hit
rate drops from ~90 % to 50 % (average translation latency per access
171 cycles instead of 3.5). Decoding runs at 24 fps. With the IOMMU out
of the way (iommu.passthrough=1), 8-bit linear references still only
reach 40 instead of 34 fps against 78 fps tiled, so the access pattern
costs more than the TLB misses.

Reconstruct into a 10-bit AFBC surface instead, as the vendor library
does for big pictures, and keep the P010 secondary output. AFBC is
lossless, and the references are read back compressed. The same 4K
stream now decodes at 60 fps (VE-bound) instead of 24, and 4K VP9
Profile 2 at 57 fps instead of 36, both with bit-exact P010 output
(whole-stream md5 against a software decode). A 32x32 tiled NV12
reconstruction would also work (49 fps) but AFBC is faster and its
size is about the same (17.3 MB vs 15.5 MB at 4K).

Native AFBC capture is unchanged.
